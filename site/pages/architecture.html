01 / The big picture

One server. Your devices.

Your Mac, Windows PC or Linux computer runs the Chirp server (chirp-server), a Python service built with Starlette and uvicorn. The Mac app and the Windows/Linux Tauri app capture audio and insert text. Android and iPhone/iPad are clients too: phones reach your computer over your private Tailscale network. Keep it on and reachable. There is no Chirp cloud or Chirp account.

02 / Speech to text

Models that run on your hardware.

Apple Silicon

Parakeet TDT 0.6B through parakeet-mlx, using MLX. English v2 is the default; multilingual v3 is a catalog alternative. Model load, warmup and decoding share one dedicated engine thread.

Intel Mac · Windows · Linux

ONNX Parakeet v2 int8 is the default. CPU execution works out of the box. Optional CUDA or DirectML providers need a compatible runtime and drivers; Chirp keeps CPU fallback. A GPU isn’t automatically faster for this model.

SenseVoice Small is an optional CPU engine through sherpa-onnx, with Silero VAD to find speech regions. It uses the same client protocol. See the backend’s language tests and setup before choosing it.

Download once, load from cache

The model catalog records pinned revisions, sizes, cached files and selections. Manage Local Models on Mac, or the server’s setup commands, make downloads and selection explicit. Refresh checks metadata; it does not silently replace a running model. Speech weights use Chirp’s local model store or a compatible existing cache; managed clean-up reuses the Hugging Face cache. Startup and inference do not download missing weights.

A conversation over WebSocket

  1. Authenticate, then start. The client opens /v1/stream and sends start with its audio format and options. The server replies ready. Audio is 16 kHz mono PCM16LE, sent as binary frames.
  2. Show words while speaking. Sequenced partial messages update the preview. MLX’s default rolling mode decodes eight-second windows and merges timestamped tokens. Optional incremental mode uses upstream cached context and one-second chunks.
  3. Stop and finalize. stop triggers the quality decode: the whole utterance, or overlapping 30-second chunks for longer MLX audio. Queued previews give way to finalization. With clean-up enabled, correcting exposes the raw result before final returns raw text, final text and timings. Default partials have an empty stable prefix because the final may revise any word.
  4. Cancel means discard. cancel suppresses the final, including during clean-up. A socket can carry successive utterances; one utterance has a 120-second server limit.

Detecting quiet and cutting segments

These are separate decisions. Parakeet uses signal-level silence checks to avoid decoding silence. Mac dictation’s Auto-stop compares the level with the room’s noise floor: two seconds of quiet by default (selectable 1–5 seconds), or ten seconds with no speech. Hold-to-talk ends when you release. Optional server --finalization segments freezes full-decoded text after at least 400 ms of low signal; it remains opt-in because it did not win consistently in the latency tests.

Live Notes keeps recording and cuts ordinary utterances instead of ending the session. On Mac and iOS, no pause cut before 12 seconds; then 0.6 seconds of quiet, relaxed to 0.3 after 30 seconds and 0.15 after 35 seconds; a hard cut at 45 seconds. Hard cuts overlap one second, with timing and text deduplication. Android Notes uses the 12 / 30 / 45-second policy with 0.6 / 0.3-second pauses. Android’s rolling keyboard dictation instead uses 45 / 70 / 90-second thresholds. The Tauri desktop currently uses fixed 45-second Notes segments.

Long dictation on Mac now rolls too: capture moves to the next utterance after 45 seconds at a suitable pause, or at 90 seconds regardless. Ordered results let one dictation last about 10 minutes without exceeding the server’s per-utterance limit. iOS and Android also support roughly 10 minutes; an iOS keyboard session can expire earlier. Segmentation keeps a dictation running; stop-on-pause ends it. On iOS and Android choose Stop dictation → When I pause and Pause length (off by default); Mac calls this Auto-stop. A hardware key held to talk ignores pauses.

Measured latency, with the conditions attached

244 ms to first partial · 107 ms stop to final. One real WebSocket simulator check on a synthetic 5.85-second clip, Parakeet MLX v2, Apple M5 Pro / 64 GiB, macOS 27.0.1, quiet-host rerun on October 17, 2026. Speech timings exclude optional LLM clean-up.

5,188 ms median · 5,540 ms p95 for structuring roughly 2,400 characters: four invented transcripts, warm Qwen3 4B 4-bit, same Mac, near-quiet load, October 20. Three of four passed the guards; these are measured request timings, not a guarantee of accepted notes.

Synthetic fixtures and specific hardware, not a cross-platform speed promise. Read the methods, contention caveats and other results.

03 / Clean-up & learning

From spoken words to usable text.

Clean-up is optional and off by default on the server. It runs on the final transcript; previews stay raw. Managed mode runs a Qwen model in a supervised helper: MLX on Apple Silicon, or GGUF through llama.cpp on Intel Mac, Windows and Linux. Endpoint mode can use an OpenAI-compatible local server, such as LM Studio or Ollama, or a deliberately chosen remote endpoint. CPU clean-up can be slow; plain dictation works without it.

In the Mac, iOS and Android apps, clean-up is on by default when it runs locally on your paired computer. Explicit choices are preserved; external destinations still need consent. On iOS, Dictate shows the current Raw/style state with a menu to change it.

Styles include Default, Chat, Email, Code, Notes and Custom. They set punctuation, formatting and tone within fixed rules. Mac and Android support per-app style choices; custom instructions and editable style prompts cannot replace the safety rules.

Output guards check word retention, negations, names, numbers and profile-specific formatting. They reject invented words, unsupported changes, assistant-like answers and truncated output. Timeouts, helper failures and rejected output keep the raw text. These are heuristic checks, not proof of identical meaning. The final preserves both versions; Mac, Android and iOS offer Use raw to choose the original after clean-up. Desktop currently has a clean-up switch without a style picker or Use raw control.

Your corrections can teach Chirp

Learn from my edits, History’s Teach Chirp and the Learned review list work on Mac, iOS and Android. Repeating the same name or technical-word correction twice activates it; Teach Chirp activates an explicit correction once. Learning requires dictation history. Mac and Android watch a verified insertion for up to 60 seconds, until focus moves or another dictation starts; small casing and punctuation edits can teach per-app style. Mac paste insertions are not watched; Android floating-mic learning needs Android 13+. Passwords and large rewrites are excluded; Android also excludes incognito fields.

On iOS, Chirp Keyboard watches only the available context around its own inserted dictation. It learns vocabulary, with no per-app style because iOS does not identify the host app. Each platform keeps an encrypted local learned store with review, approval, undo and deletion. Active items travel with dictation in start.learned, including to a configured external clean-up endpoint. Conservative respelling also works with clean-up off. Mac learning · Android learning · iOS learning.

Private session on Mac and Android skips new history, learning and learned context until screen lock, one hour or app exit. iOS calls its persistent switch Pause dictation history: it also pauses learning and learned context, but stays on until you turn it off. Existing history is kept.

04 / Live Notes

A transcript becomes a document.

Capture runs in segments, with each completed transcript kept in order. On Mac and phones, the next socket takes capture while the old segment finalizes. A stateless /v1/notes/structure request sends a transcript window and an outline to the configured clean-up model. It returns Markdown headings, bullets, tasks, decisions and questions. Without structuring, you still have the transcript and an editor.

/v1/notes/reorganize regroups existing notes. On Mac and phones, new generated text has an AI-owned tail: editing it makes it yours. Version checks discard stale Organize results when you have changed the document. Mac and Android also offer guarded voice edits through /v1/notes/edit. The desktop saves each transcript segment before optional structuring and limits Organize to 3,500 characters, with a version check and Undo.

The recording device owns the library

  • Mac: local JSON files containing the Markdown document, metadata and transcript in Application Support. Private folders and files (0700 / 0600), not encrypted note content. No automatic sync or Spotlight indexing.
  • Android: one AES-GCM sealed file per note and a sealed index, with an Android Keystore key in credential-encrypted app storage; excluded from backup and device transfer.
  • iPhone / iPad: an AES-GCM sealed library in Application Support, protected by a device-only Keychain key and complete iOS file protection; excluded from backup and Spotlight.
  • Windows / Linux: local JSON documents and index in the app’s data directory. No content encryption; Linux uses private file modes and Windows checks a protected user + SYSTEM ACL.

Libraries save as you go, support local search and Markdown export, and keep notes until deletion by default. Recently Deleted retains removed notes for 30 days. Export creates a separate copy.

New note creates a blank typed document on Mac and Android, without opening the microphone or contacting your computer. Record starts audio capture separately. The Windows/Linux desktop also creates blank notes; the iOS library still starts through Record.

Keep audio with notes is opt-in on Mac, Android and iOS. It keeps short mono AAC clips for transcript replay. Phone clips are sealed with the notes key and decrypted in memory; Mac clips use private file permissions. Removing kept audio preserves text. Windows/Linux desktop has no kept audio or replay.

Re-transcribe the audio you kept

On Mac, Android and iOS, Re-transcribe… asks every time and shows duration, clip count, model and destination before sending a stopped note’s kept clips only to your own Chirp server. Clips decode in memory and use the authenticated stream with preview inference disabled; live dictation has priority. Audio and transcripts are not kept on the server unless you enabled debug retention, which the sheet warns about. A configured external clean-up endpoint can receive text, never audio.

Results apply together after the run, preserving names, existing speaker turns and notes edits. Missing or failed clips keep their text; Cancel discards staged results. The previous transcript stays with the note for Undo and Restore previous transcript. Also rebuild the notes is a separate, undoable choice, off by default. Allow re-transcribing hides the actions and cancels a running one when off. Use Re-transcribe.

05 / Speaker detection

Who said what?

Label speakers is on by default on Mac, iPhone/iPad and Android and can be turned off. It groups voices and aligns their turns with the transcript; a label is not a guarantee of someone’s identity. Similar voices can merge, and one voice can split.

Mac: in the app

Streaming Sortformer through FluidAudio / CoreML diarizes on the Mac. If its 106 MB model is missing, the first labelled recording fetches it by itself, once, with a status line and a Turn off, and never asks first. With optional call-audio capture, the microphone side is You and the call side is diarized on this same Mac. “Remote participants” describes the people on the call; their diarization does not run in a remote service.

Phones: on your server

Android and iOS request WeSpeaker ResNet34-LM embeddings from your server. Voiced windows are clustered within a segment and matched to earlier speakers in that note. Your computer fetches the pinned, hash-checked model (about 27 MB) by itself the first time, with a status line and a Turn off; recording never waits for it.

Phone speaker comparison state lives only in server memory, never in logs or returned as voiceprints. It expires after six idle hours, when the 32-note bound is exceeded, or on restart. Names, merges and text turns belong to the saved note. Name speakers lets you name everyone after Stop; Same person as merges a split and Separate undoes a merge. On Mac and Android, This turn is… fixes just one sentence’s speaker without renaming the person throughout the note. iOS single-turn reassignment is in progress. Structuring receives Name: … attribution so tasks can retain their owner.

All three apps share one Saved voices library kept on your Chirp server: naming a person with Remember voice stores a name and a voiceprint there (at most 50, owner-only file) once there are 20 seconds of speech, and every paired device then recognizes that person in later notes, shown with a ✓ and a Not X to undo it. The Mac sends bounded clips of settled turns and the phones send only a temporary session reference; the server compares with the same WeSpeaker model. One server-wide Remember voices switch turns saving and recognizing off, and Forget deletes voices without touching the names already in notes. Phones keep no voiceprints, and the Mac’s older Keychain voices are imported once. Windows and Linux desktop do not currently label speakers or capture call audio.

06 / Input on each platform

Same engine. Different ways in.

Mac
A native Swift menu-bar app. The hotkey state machine handles hold and toggle; a modifier event tap requires Accessibility. A HUD shows live words and clean-up state. Insertion uses Accessibility, with paste or copy fallback; terminals and Chromium/Electron apps prefer paste.
Android
A HeliBoard-based full keyboard with a voice strip. Or keep your keyboard and use the floating mic through Accessibility. Quick Dictate has a Quick Settings tile and a launcher widget; plain-text Share can create or append to a note. Tile/widget dictation auto-copies the final, while the floating mic copies only when you tap Copy.
iPhone / iPad
A full typing keyboard with suggestions and a steady-height dictation row. iOS does not let the extension open the microphone: the app starts a timed audio session, then you return to the original app. Full Access enables the shared session. On iPad with Magic Keyboard, hold Right ⌥ inside Chirp; elsewhere Shortcuts + Full Keyboard Access offers toggle-only dictation, followed by paste. Notes has Record, Pause/Resume and Stop actions in Shortcuts and Control Center. Live Activities expose session controls; the Notes activity can show title, time and state, never the note body.
Windows / Linux
A Tauri app with native CPAL audio capture and a shared HUD. Windows inserts with Unicode SendInput or a clipboard paste. Linux X11 uses XTest; Wayland uses a compositor-approved Global Shortcuts portal and wtype where supported, otherwise copy-to-paste. Windows still needs a real-PC acceptance pass; Wayland behavior depends on your compositor.

Spoken commands on Mac, iOS Chirp Keyboard and Android convert a trailing “new line” or “new paragraph” into line breaks when enabled. This is an optional formatting feature; Windows/Linux desktop does not have it yet.

iOS is early too: simulator end-to-end checks and a first iPad install are recorded; iPhone hardware acceptance and real microphone/background checks remain pending. Read the platform setup guides.

07 / Privacy & security

Know where the data goes.

The server binds to loopback. Tailscale Serve exposes it privately to your tailnet, with TLS; Mac pairing refuses public Funnel exposure. Bearer authentication happens before audio is accepted. Pairing QR codes carry a secret token: keep them private. Named per-device tokens are independently revocable; the server stores their SHA-256 digests rather than the original tokens.

Client credentials use the platform’s protected storage: Mac reads its private token file; Android seals credentials with Keystore; iOS uses Keychain; desktop uses Windows Credential Manager or Linux Secret Service. Tokens are not URL parameters on stream requests.

Routine logs contain counts, timings, fixed status/error codes and session identifiers, never audio, transcripts, notes, prompts or credentials. Dictation audio is transient in normal operation. Libraries, optional history and learning stay on the client; the server’s Notes endpoints do not keep documents. Mac history defaults on for 30 days; Android defaults on for 7 days; iOS stays off until enabled, then defaults to 7 days. All three offer 1, 7 and 30 days; Mac also offers 90 days and Forever. Explicit choices are preserved. Turning history off stops new entries and offers keep or delete; retained entries stay readable until expiry or Delete history. Optional kept Notes audio follows the storage rules above.

Two deliberate settings change the path. Debug retention keeps WAV and transcript files locally on the server, expiring after three days by default, and clients show a warning. An external clean-up endpoint receives final dictation text and supplied context; Notes requests send their transcript windows or notes text when enabled for that host. It receives no audio. The clients show the destination, and Notes processing needs consent for that host.

With local models, speech and text processing stays on your devices. Speech and clean-up model downloads are explicit; the first labelled note automatically downloads the speaker model once, with progress and Turn off. Saved voices are a shared server library, not note sync: devices using different Chirp servers do not share voices. No Chirp telemetry or note-library sync is built in. Android’s optional phone-recognizer fallback is a separate on-device service from Google or the phone maker, labeled on phone, under that provider’s terms.
