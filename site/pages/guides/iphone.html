Use Chirp on iPhone and iPad

A native app plus a dictation keyboard for iOS and iPadOS 18. Like Android, it sends your voice to the Chirp server on your computer. There’s no App Store or TestFlight build yet — both are coming soon; this guide is the personal-install route today.

Needs: full Xcode, an Apple ID, TailscaleStatus: early
Early: first iPad install verified.

The app, keyboard and widgets build and pass their tests on iPhone and iPad simulators, including a test that dictates through the real keyboard. An iPad personal install was verified on October 25, 2026; iPhone installation, microphone quality and Live Notes on hardware are still unverified. Expect rough edges — and please report what you find. Packaged iOS builds are coming soon; there is no App Store or TestFlight download yet.

Before you start

  • Chirp running on your computer (Mac, Windows or Linux guide) and Tailscale on that computer and the iPhone or iPad, signed in to the same account (how).
  • Full Xcode from the Mac App Store, opened once so it finishes installing. The Command Line Tools alone can't build iOS apps.
  • An Apple ID signed in to Xcode (Xcode → Settings → Accounts). Free Personal Team profiles expire after seven days, requiring another build. App Groups and shared Keychain capabilities are not guaranteed on free accounts; check the actual provisioning profiles.

Build and install

  1. Check the toolchain 
    ios/scripts/toolchain.sh

    If it says BLOCKED, point the tools at Xcode: sudo xcode-select -s /Applications/Xcode.app.

  2. Set your team

    Copy ios/Config/Local.example.xcconfig to ios/Config/Local.xcconfig and set DEVELOPMENT_TEAM to your team ID (shown in Xcode → Settings → Accounts). Keep the same signing team, App Group and keychain access group on the app, keyboard and widgets. This file stays on your Mac.

  3. Open the project

    Open ios/Chirp.xcodeproj. For the ChirpIOS, ChirpKeyboard and ChirpWidgets targets, choose your team under Signing.

  4. Complete the README’s iPhone and iPad simulator gates

    Before a personal hardware install, follow the build, unit and UI checks in the iOS README on both device families.

  5. Run it on your device

    Plug in the iPhone, trust the computer and turn on Developer Mode (Settings → Privacy & Security). Pick the device and the ChirpIOS scheme, then press ⌘R. Same for an iPad — it's one universal app.

The iOS README also describes simulator test runs to do before installing on hardware.

Pair with your computer

  1. Show the code on the computer

    In Chirp for Mac → Settings → Devices → Pair a Phone… On a Windows or Linux server, run chirp-server pair there instead.

  2. Scan it

    In Chirp on the iPhone, use Pair your computer during onboarding, or Settings → Connection, and tap Scan pairing QR. Chirp checks the connection before saving, then shows which computer you paired.

The iPhone app only accepts private tailnet addresses, so it can never be pointed at a public server.

Turn on Chirp Keyboard

  1. Add it in Settings

    Tap Open Chirp in Settings in the app, then Keyboards: turn on Chirp Keyboard and Allow Full Access.

  2. Open it once

    In any app, tap the globe key until Chirp Keyboard appears. The app's checklist ticks the step off.

Why Full Access?

iOS never gives keyboards the microphone. The Chirp app records instead and hands the text to the keyboard through an encrypted shared mailbox — that's what Full Access allows. Password fields always use Apple's keyboard.

Chirp Keyboard is a full keyboard, so you can leave it on: QWERTY in Apple’s look with shift and caps lock, the 123 and symbol pages, long-press accents, a double space for a full stop, the space bar as a cursor and suggestions with careful autocorrect from the spell checker on your phone (press Delete right after a correction to undo it). Its new top row keeps suggestions in the middle, with dictation options and the mic at the edges; its height stays steady while listening and polishing. There is no emoji page yet; the globe key takes you to Apple’s emoji keyboard.

Dictate

  • In the app: the Dictate screen records directly. Afterwards you get Copy, Share and, if clean-up changed something, Use raw.
  • In other apps: tap Start keyboard session in Chirp (or use the Action Button, Control Center or Shortcuts), switch to the app you want, pick Chirp Keyboard with the globe key and tap the mic in its strip. Type around your dictation with the same keyboard.
  • On iPad with a keyboard: in Chirp, hold Right ⌥ to dictate and let go to insert, into the field you are typing in or onto Dictate. Esc cancels, and ⌘Return and ⌘. also start and cancel. Change the key, switch to press-to-start-and-stop or turn it off under Settings → Dictation → Keyboard shortcut.
  • Other apps with a hardware keyboard: iPadOS has no system-wide keys for apps, and Chirp Keyboard doesn't receive hardware keys, so a key outside Chirp can only start and stop (no hold). Make a Shortcuts shortcut with Toggle Chirp dictation, then give it a key under Settings → Accessibility → Keyboards & Typing → Full Keyboard Access → Commands. Press it once to start and again to stop, then ⌘V to paste. A keyboard session must be running.

Pauses, corrections and history

Stop dictation in Settings → Dictation offers When I tap (the default) and When I pause, with a choice of Pause length. Stop always works; a hardware key held to talk ignores pauses. Rolling dictation runs for about 10 minutes, but the keyboard session’s own timer can end it earlier. Spoken commands in Chirp Keyboard converts a trailing “new line” or “new paragraph” to a line break.

Learn from my edits, Teach Chirp in History and the Learned review list now work on iPhone and iPad. Learning needs dictation history, which is off by default. Teach a correction once in History, or make the same small word correction twice after a Chirp Keyboard dictation. The keyboard can observe only its available text context; iOS provides no reliable host-app identity, so this learns vocabulary, not per-app style. Learned words are encrypted on this device and active terms accompany dictation to your computer and any configured clean-up provider.

Pause dictation history in Privacy & history also pauses learning and sending learned context. It stays on until you turn it off and keeps existing history. Mac and Android use a temporary Private session instead, ending on lock, after one hour or on app exit.

Notes from Shortcuts and Control Center

Use Record note with Chirp, Pause or resume note with Chirp and Stop note with Chirp in Shortcuts or add their controls to Control Center. Starting a recording opens Chirp; pause releases the microphone. Settings → Keyboard shortcuts lists the Notes keys alongside dictation shortcuts.

Speaker labels are on by default. Name speakers names everyone after Stop, and Remember voice uses the single Saved voices library on your computer, recognised by other devices paired to that server. This turn is on a chip moves one sentence to the right person. New note, beside Record, types a blank note without a microphone or your computer. Re-transcribe… sends a stopped note’s kept audio to your computer again. Read the Notes guide.

About the microphone indicator

During a keyboard session the app keeps the microphone ready so the keyboard can start instantly, so iOS shows the orange mic dot the whole time. Audio between dictations is thrown away immediately and nothing leaves the phone. Sessions end after 5 minutes by default; End session in the app, keyboard or Live Activity turns the mic off.
