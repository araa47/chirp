Take notes

Leave the mic open for a meeting, a lecture or thinking out loud. Chirp writes Markdown notes while you talk (headings, bullets, to-dos, decisions and open questions) next to the full transcript, and keeps every note in a library you can search.

For the models, segment boundaries and platform differences, read the architecture.

Start and stop

  1. Press ⌃⌥N

    From any Mac app. It always starts a new note and records, and the Notes window comes forward without taking focus from the app you’re in. Or open Notes Library… and press Record.

  2. Talk

    Speech is transcribed in short chunks. At most every 15 seconds the local model turns the newest stretch into notes. Earlier sections stay put, so nothing shifts while you read.

  3. Stop

    Press Stop in the recording control in the toolbar (or ⇧⌘R), or choose the first row of the menu-bar menu, which reads ◉ Recording “…” 03:12 — Stop. ⇧⌘P pauses and resumes. Chirp finishes the last bit and tidies the notes once more.

Prefer to type? In the Mac Notes window, New Note (⌘N) opens a blank document without recording. Android has New note in the library toolbar (Ctrl+N), and iPhone and iPad have it beside Record at the bottom of the library (⌘N with a keyboard). All of them work without a microphone or a reachable computer, and a note that is recording keeps recording; Record is a separate action (⇧⌘R on iPad).

Notes need the clean-up model.

Structuring uses the same local model as clean-up. Without it, Notes still works as a live transcript and a plain editor. Download one under Settings → Advanced → Manage Local Models….

Notes and dictation take turns on a Mac: while a note records, the dictation hotkey waits, and a phone dictating to the same Mac may be told it’s busy.

Edit while it writes

A note is one Markdown document you can type in at any time: before, during or after recording.

  • It reads like a document. Markdown is hidden except on the line you’re editing: headings without ##, bold without **, to-dos as boxes you can tick. View ▸ Show Markdown shows every character.
  • The softly tinted block at the end is what Chirp may still rewrite as you keep talking. Edit anything in it and it becomes yours: the tint goes away and Chirp never touches it again.
  • Your text is safe. Updates never overwrite your typing or move your cursor, and each one is a single undo step.
  • Rename by typing over the large title. Your name wins over the one Chirp suggests.

Your library

Every note saves itself as you go; there is no Save step. The sidebar lists notes newest first, grouped Today, Yesterday, Previous 7 Days and so on, each with a snippet, its length and how many to-dos are still open.

  • Search (⌥⌘F) looks through titles, notes and transcripts and highlights the matches.
  • Continue recording on a finished note records more into it, after a quiet “Continued” divider. ⌃⌥N still always starts a new recording.
  • Delete (⌘⌫) moves a note to Recently Deleted for 30 days, with Undo. A note that’s recording can’t be deleted until you stop it.
  • Recovered: if the Mac crashes or loses power mid-recording, the note is simply in the library next time, marked Recovered.
  • How long notes are kept is up to you: Settings → Notes → Delete notes older than, Never by default. Notes stay on this Mac, readable only by your user account, and are never synced or indexed by Spotlight.

Replay what was said

The transcript sits in an inspector beside the note: press ⌥⌘T or the sidebar button at the right of the toolbar. It reads as paragraphs, each under its time.

Turn on Settings → Notes → Keep audio with notes and each spoken segment is kept with its note. A play button then sits beside each transcript paragraph; the paragraph lights up while it plays. It’s off by default. Kept audio stays on the recording device unless you choose Re-transcribe and confirm. Settings shows how much space it takes; deleting audio keeps the text.

Re-transcribe from kept audio

Available on Mac, Android, iPhone and iPad. Open a stopped note that kept audio and choose Re-transcribe…. Use it after changing the speech model on your computer, or to try a segment that failed. A transcript row can transcribe just one clip; gap rows on the phones offer Try again.

Chirp asks every time. The sheet shows the audio length, clip count, destination and model before sending anything. Audio goes only to your own Chirp server, on a Mac, Linux computer or Windows PC. It is processed in memory and not kept there by default. If debug recordings are enabled, the sheet warns that your computer will keep them; an external clean-up provider can receive text, never audio.

Names, speaker assignments and your notes text survive. Also rebuild the notes is a separate choice, off by default, because it replaces the body. Cancel discards staged transcript changes; successful runs offer Undo and later Restore previous transcript. Clips without audio or with failed requests keep their old text. Turn off Settings → Notes → Allow re-transcribing to hide the actions.

Organize and speakers

Every few minutes while you record, Chirp regroups recent notes: duplicate headings merge, and tasks, decisions and questions are collected, without dropping or adding anything (your Chirp server checks). The ✦ Smart notes menu in the toolbar has Organize Now (also ⌥⌘R), a review of what changed with Restore previous, and the switches for Structure notes and Organize automatically.

Speaker labels are on by default on Mac, Android and iPhone/iPad. Person 1, Person 2… chips mark each turn in the transcript. Click or tap a chip to rename a person, choose Same person as to merge two, or separate them again. Names appear in exports and can inform task ownership. The speaker model downloads by itself the first time, once (about 106 MB on the Mac, about 27 MB on your server for the phones), with a status line and a Turn off in place of a prompt; recording never waits for it, and labels start once it is ready. Turn Label speakers off in the ✦ Smart notes menu or Settings → Notes on the Mac, or from a note’s menu or Settings → Notes on the phones. After you stop, a Name speakers sheet lets you name everyone at once. Kept audio still plays by segment.

Saved voices. Tick Remember voice when you name someone and your Chirp server keeps their voiceprint in one Saved voices library, shared by every device paired to it: name a person on your phone and your Mac, Android phone or iPhone recognizes them in later notes (a ✓ on the chip; Not Maya undoes a wrong match). Phones keep no voiceprints of their own, and the Mac’s old Keychain voices move to the server once. Remember voices of people I name is one server-wide switch, and Forget removes voices from the server but keeps the names already in your notes. The Mac labels on the Mac itself; Android and iPhone/iPad ask your computer. Mac Notes details. Android Notes details. iPhone and iPad Notes details.

Fix one sentence’s speaker. Open that turn’s chip menu and choose This turn is, then the correct person or New person. This works on Mac, Android, iPhone and iPad. It changes just that turn, and later labels and Re-transcribe keep your choice; Rename and Same person as affect the person throughout the note. Speaker labels and saved matches can be wrong, so review them.

Export

  • Copy Markdown (⇧⌘C) copies the note as edited.
  • Export Markdown… (⇧⌘S) writes the note and its transcript to a .md file you choose. Tick Keep saving changes to this file to keep that file up to date. The note stays in your library either way.

Notes on Android

Chirp for Android opens on its Notes library, and its notes are structured by your computer over your tailnet.

  • New note opens a blank typed note. Record starts recording; it keeps going with the screen off, with Pause and Stop in its notification. The Notes Quick Settings tile starts and stops a recording too.
  • On an unfolded Fold the list and the note sit side by side. Elsewhere, tap a note to open it and the Transcript chip to see what was said.
  • Search, swipe a note away to delete it (with Undo), or long-press to select several. Tap the title to rename it.
  • Notes are kept automatically and encrypted on the phone. Keep audio with notes (Settings → Notes, off by default) lets you replay segments from the transcript. Label speakers is on by default.

Notes on iPhone and iPad

Notes is the app’s start tab. Record into a transcript and structured Markdown using your computer. On iPad notes and transcript sit side by side; on iPhone switch between them with the picker. Notes are saved as you go in an encrypted local library, with search, Recently Deleted, Label speakers (on by default) and Saved voices, and optional Keep audio with notes for segment replay. Shortcuts offers Record note with Chirp, Pause or resume note with Chirp and Stop note with Chirp; add the matching controls in Control Center for quick access. Starting a note opens Chirp. The iOS app’s device recording and background behavior are still early.
